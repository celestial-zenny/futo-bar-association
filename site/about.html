About the association

About the FUTO Bar Association

The FBA is the body of intending barristers at the Federal University of Technology Owerri. This page sets out what the association is for, in the words of its own constitution.

The preamble

The constitution of the association opens with the reason the members brought it into being. They formed the FBA, it reads, in order to form a more perfect union, build a just and humane society, establish a Government that shall embody our ideals and aspirations, promote the common good of the association, establish justice, ensure domestic tranquillity, conserve and develop our patrimony, ensure law and order and secure the blessings of liberty to ourselves, for Posterity — and, in accordance with the objectives of students' unionism, they enacted and gave themselves the constitution that governs the association today.

Name and standing

Article 1 of the constitution names the association FUTO Bar Association (FBA). It is explicit that no purpose or objective of the FBA may be in conflict with the constitution of the Students' Union Government or with the laws of the school — the association operates inside the SUG framework, under its Judiciary, not alongside it.

Aims and objectives

Article 2 lists what the association exists to do:

  1. To be organised consistently with the aims and objectives of instituting legality amongst students of FUTO.
  2. To preserve, protect and defend the rule of law, in accordance with the Human Rights Declaration of the Federal Republic of Nigeria and the constitution of the Students' Union Government, among the students of FUTO.
  3. To provide an opportunity for the students of FUTO to be represented and to actively participate in legality.
  4. To create a veritable forum to promote and develop physical, social, mental and intellectual prowess.
  5. To encourage and maintain a cordial, peaceful and loving relationship amongst all members, irrespective of differences.
  6. To condemn and oppose all forms of injustice, oppression and victimisation through constitutional means.
  7. To protect and maintain the constitution and the image of the members.

Conduct expected of members

Articles 10 and 11 set the professional standard. A Code of Conduct Panel of five members monitors the out-of-court behaviour of members and investigates allegations of misconduct, including against members of the Executive Council. Members are required to wear white on black for meetings, court sittings and other recognised functions of the association; a member who has collected consultation and retention fees may not refuse to appear for their client, and no member may abandon a case being handled without giving the court and the client forty-eight hours' notice before the next sitting.

Training through the SSPS

Legal literacy is taught rather than assumed. The SUG School of Paralegal Studies runs the Level 1 I-Barrister classes under the mantra impacting legal knowledge into our technological minds, with the stated goal of mastering the basics of law, student rights and campus political entry. Six courses run across the week — judicial analysis and litigation, jurisprudence and constitutional law, professional ethics and advocacy, the legislative process and time management, advocacy and activism, and leadership.

Every hand-out is available to download, and the governing documents are published in full.

  • Founded on

  • Governed by

  • Sits under

    The Judiciary of the FUTO Students' Union Government, at the Federal University of Technology, P.M.B. 1526, Owerri, Imo State.

  • Trains through

    The SUG School of Paralegal Studies (SSPS) and its I-Barrister classes, with weekly notes and moot court preparation.
